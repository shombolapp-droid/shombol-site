How to spot an investment scam

People abroad are easy targets, because it is hard to check things at home. A few simple rules avoid most traps.

Facts checked: 20 November 2026Reading time: 3 min

The government's highest-paying bond gives 12% a year. If someone promises a "guaranteed" 3% to 5% a month, that is 36% to 60% a year. No safe place pays that.

Warning signs

  • "Guaranteed monthly profit": any promise far above government rates is a red flag.
  • Cash to a person: government bonds and Sanchayapatra are sold only by banks, post offices and the National Savings Bureau. Never by an individual or an "agent".
  • Pressure: "last chance today" or "limited seats" means stop.
  • Through someone you know: many scams come through a relative or someone from the village. Check anyway, however good the relationship.
  • Stock market "insider tips": stay away from anyone promising a sure price rise.

Three checks before you pay

  1. Who is selling?If it is not a bank, a post office or the National Savings Bureau, do not pay.
  2. What paper will you get?Always get a receipt, a certificate or an online registration number.
  3. Which account is the money going to?Only an account in the institution's name. Never a personal account.

Shombol never takes money from anyone and sells no investment. If someone asks for money in Shombol's name, it is a scam.

If it happens to you

  • Keep every piece of evidence: messages, receipts, phone numbers, bank transfers.
  • File a complaint or a GD at a police station at home, with your family's help.
  • Tell the bank you sent the money through straight away.
  • Do not feel ashamed. Warning others protects more people.

Questions people ask

Does Shombol take or invest my money?

No. Shombol is an educational platform. We never take anyone's money and sell no investment.

Can I trust an offer of 5% profit a month?

No. That is 60% a year, while the highest government rate is 12% a year.
